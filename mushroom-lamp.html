Mushroom Lamp

A playful polygonal lamp exploring laser-cut structure, layered material, translucent vellum, color, and physical assembly.

Laser Cutting Object Design Material Study Lighting Prototype Assembly
role

Concept development, Illustrator file work, laser cutting, material testing, assembly, painting, and documentation.

goal

Create a playful light object that combines a natural mushroom form with geometric construction and layered material effects.

materials

MDF, cardstock, vellum, acrylic paint, adhesive, matte clear coat, and lighting hardware.

Concept

The project started as an exploration of dimensional objects inspired by everyday objects, nature, and character-like forms. The final direction became a polygonal mushroom lamp that combined playful proportions with a structured laser-cut build.

Material direction

I wanted the lamp to feel natural but slightly futuristic. The design used MDF for the structural stem, red cardstock for the outer mushroom cap, vellum for a frosted translucent underside, and green painted base layers to ground the object visually.

Interaction

The object was designed around light diffusion and visual character. The vellum layer creates a frosted effect, while the polygonal shape, arms, legs, and layered base make the lamp feel more like a small designed object than a standard fixture.

Prototype

The first test cut used clear acrylic and revealed measurement and angle issues. That prototype helped me understand where the file needed to change so the edges could connect more cleanly and the lamp could assemble more like a puzzle.

Iteration

I revised the design to improve the stem, add arms and legs, create a larger opening for the light wire, and adjust the tab system. Sanding the MDF corners also helped the pieces fit together without damaging the tabs.

Final assembly

The final lamp used an octagonal MDF stem, foldable cardstock and vellum sections, a three-tier green base, acrylic paint, matte clear coat, Mod Podge for paper layers, and superglue for stronger structural connections.

What I learned

This project taught me how much physical prototyping depends on test fitting, material behavior, and small assembly details. The final object changed significantly from the original concept, which made the process feel more successful rather than less.

Next iteration

If I built another version, I would use red vellum instead of cardstock for a more transparent cap, refine the internal lighting mount, and model the full assembly in 3D earlier to predict angle and tab issues before cutting.
